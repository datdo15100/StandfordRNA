\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The Stanford RNA 3D Folding competition asks a system to map an RNA sequence to at
most five three-dimensional structures and evaluates the best structure using
TM-score. This thesis uses that clearly defined objective as an experimental setting
for a broader question: how does each major decision in a competitive RNA 3D pipeline
affect final structural prediction quality? The starting point is the publicly
released pipeline of John, a member of the winning team. The public method is treated
as an inherited baseline, not as an exact reconstruction of the winning submission.
John and the thesis pipeline are first placed under common data, temporal, exclusion,
candidate-budget, and evaluation rules. Groups of components are then changed in a
controlled sequence: template-based modelling, candidate-source allocation, and
geometric refinement.

Three primary hypotheses were preregistered. H1 compares the retained thesis TBM with
J-controlled TBM using five raw candidates. H2 compares five template candidates with
three template and two direct DRfold2 candidates while keeping the budget fixed at
five. H3 compares Geometry Refinement with a simple source-and-backbone smoother on
the same raw candidates. H1 and H2 use best-of-five TM-score. H3 uses sliding-window
RMSD over nine residues, or SW-RMSD9, as the primary local metric and requires a
predeclared TM-score preservation margin. RNA targets are the statistical units, and
regenerated MMseqs sequence-similarity clusters are the dependence blocks.

Development experiments on 20 exposed RNAs selected the final components but did not
establish generalization. The frozen method was therefore evaluated once on 97
confirmatory RNAs in 86 sequence-similarity clusters. H1 failed: retained thesis TBM
reduced mean TM-score by 0.0255 relative to J-controlled TBM, a 6.358\% decrease, with
a cluster-bootstrap 95\% confidence interval of $[-0.0471,-0.0072]$. H2 passed:
replacing two of five template slots with direct DRfold2 candidates increased mean
TM-score by 0.0342, or 9.098\%, with interval $[0.0155,0.0543]$. The mixed bank also
had lower self-similarity and fewer near-duplicate pairs, supporting source
complementarity rather than DRfold2-only superiority.

H3 also passed. Selected Geometry reduced SW-RMSD9 by 0.0205~\angstrom{} relative to
Simple, or 0.473\%, with interval $[0.0141,0.0266]$. It also increased bank-level
TM-score by 0.00218, or 0.532\%, and passed the preregistered $-0.005$ preservation
margin. Supporting metrics were mixed: SW-RMSD15 improved, global \cprime{} RMSD and
candidate TM were inconclusive, and \cprime{}-lDDT decreased. The final contribution
is therefore specific. Fixed-budget candidate-source diversification provides the
main confirmed TM-score gain, while Geometry provides a smaller confirmed
SW-RMSD9 improvement beyond simple smoothing. The thesis TBM replacement is a
negative result and is not claimed as an improvement over the inherited controlled
baseline.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RNA three-dimensional structure, template-based modelling,
DRfold2, geometric refinement, TM-score, SW-RMSD, temporal leakage.

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background and motivation}

RNA is not only a sequence of A, U, G, and C. The chain folds into motifs and
three-dimensional interactions that support regulation, catalysis, and molecular
recognition \cite{cao_rna_functions,tinoco_folding,butcher_tertiary}. Experimental
methods such as X-ray crystallography, nuclear magnetic resonance, and cryogenic
electron microscopy provide high-value reference structures. However, experimental
structure determination remains costly and technically demanding, and many RNAs do
not have a known close structural relative \cite{zhang_experimental_review,ma_cryoem}.
Prediction from sequence is therefore both a scientific and an engineering problem.

An RNA 3D prediction system can obtain information from several sources.
Template-based modelling, abbreviated TBM, finds an RNA with a known structure and
transfers its geometry to the query. A pretrained model learns patterns from existing
data and can produce structures even when direct template evidence is weak. A refiner
receives an existing structure and adjusts its coordinates to reduce local
irregularities. These stages solve different problems. A single end-to-end score does
not identify which stage caused a change.

The Stanford RNA 3D Folding competition offers a useful experimental setting. For each
target, a system receives the sequence and returns at most five predictions. The score
is the highest TM-score across the five predictions and all accepted native
conformations. This objective rewards both the accuracy of an individual candidate and
the coverage of the complete candidate bank. An independent candidate can add value
even when its average quality is lower, provided that it solves a target missed by the
other source.

\section{Research problem}

John's public notebooks provide an important competitive baseline associated with the
winning team \cite{kaggle_top1,kaggle_hybrid}. Nevertheless, subtracting the score of
one public notebook from another submission cannot attribute a gain to one component.
The systems may differ simultaneously in their template library, temporal filtering,
self-exclusion, pretrained model, fallback, and refinement. The Kaggle leaderboard
also provides only an aggregate score on hidden targets rather than paired per-target
evidence.

This thesis addresses the attribution problem through controlled decomposition. John
and the thesis pipeline are first placed on the same target set, template snapshot,
temporal and self-exclusion rules, candidate budget, and evaluator. Each main
experiment then replaces one coherent group of components: TBM, candidate-source
allocation, or refinement. Local controlled experiments are used for attribution.
Kaggle is reserved for external evidence about the complete frozen system.

The study also separates four types of benefit. An accuracy contribution changes
native structural accuracy. An availability contribution produces candidates where a
method previously failed. A diversity contribution reduces redundant hypotheses in a
fixed candidate bank. An engineering safeguard reduces leakage or execution risk
without necessarily increasing accuracy. These categories prevent a robustness feature
from being reported as a demonstrated score improvement.

\section{Research questions}

The overarching question is:

\begin{quote}
How do the major stages of an RNA 3D structure prediction pipeline contribute to the
TM-score objective used in the Kaggle competition?
\end{quote}

Three specific questions define the experimental sequence:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5cm,style=nextline]
  \item[RQ1.] Which changes to the inherited TBM pipeline affect best-of-five TM-score
  under a common controlled protocol, and what roles do retrieval, ranking, and
  reconstruction play?
  \item[RQ2.] Given a fixed budget of five predictions, does replacing two template
  candidates with candidates from an independent pretrained predictor improve
  structural coverage and best-of-five TM-score?
  \item[RQ3.] Can Geometry Refinement improve local \cprime{} structural accuracy
  beyond simple smoothing while preserving the global fold measured by TM-score?
\end{description}

\section{Contributions and scope}

The thesis does not claim that the full pipeline was invented from scratch. TBM and
the hybrid direction are inherited from John's public work. The scientific
contribution is a controlled, auditable analysis of changes made to that baseline.
Each claim is tied to the experiment that can support it. A component is described as
a demonstrated contribution only when it passes its preregistered gate. A component
that only reduces redundancy or prevents leakage is described as a safeguard.

The study contributes a 5,135-row data provenance ledger, an exact reproduction of the
production geometry prior, a shared controlled template database, a partial but
executable reconstruction of the public John pipeline, fixed-budget source-allocation
experiments, and a same-candidate refinement factorial. It also provides deterministic
cluster-aware inference and a final pre-opening receipt that freezes the target
manifest, code, data, checkpoints, priors, fallback rules, metrics, and statistics.

The local representation contains one \cprime{} coordinate per nucleotide. The main
competition objective is best-of-five TM-score. Local refinement is assessed with
SW-RMSD9 and supporting metrics. Diagnostics directly optimized by Geometry, including
angle likelihood, torsion likelihood, clash count, kink count, and radius-of-gyration
deviation, are not treated as independent proof of native accuracy.

\chapter{Background and Problem Definition}

\section{RNA coordinate representation}

Each sequence record has a target identifier, an RNA sequence, a temporal cutoff, and
descriptive metadata. Each label record represents one residue and contains its base,
position, and three-dimensional coordinates. The competition uses the sugar atom
\cprime{} as the representative point for each nucleotide. A prediction for an RNA of
length $L$ is therefore a matrix

\begin{equation}
X\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times 3},
\end{equation}

where row $i$ stores $(x_i,y_i,z_i)$ for residue $i$. A target may have several valid
experimental conformations. The evaluator must therefore compare the candidate bank
with all accepted references.

\section{TM-score and best-of-five evaluation}

TM-score measures structural similarity after finding a suitable rotation and
translation. In a general form,

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TM}(X,Y)=\max_{\mathcal{A}}
\frac{1}{L_{\mathrm{ref}}}
\sum_{i=1}^{L_{\mathrm{ali}}}
\frac{1}{1+\left(d_i/d_0(L_{\mathrm{ref}})\right)^2},
\end{equation}

where $d_i$ is the distance between an aligned pair of \cprime{} atoms after
superposition, and $d_0$ depends on reference length \cite{tm_score,usalign}. A close
pair contributes a value near one. The contribution decreases smoothly as the distance
increases. Unlike ordinary RMSD, a small number of large local errors do not dominate
the entire score through a squared penalty. TM-score therefore emphasizes the global
fold.

If target $q$ has five predictions $X_q^{(1)},\ldots,X_q^{(5)}$ and $R_q$ accepted
native conformations, the target score is

\begin{equation}
S_q=\max_{k\in\{1,\ldots,5\}}
\max_{r\in\{1,\ldots,R_q\}}
\mathrm{TM}\left(X_q^{(k)},Y_q^{(r)}\right).
\end{equation}

For example, if the best native TM-scores of the five predictions are 0.31, 0.42,
0.38, 0.57, and 0.44, the target receives 0.57. A second candidate that closely
duplicates the first may add no value. A candidate from another source can increase
the score if it covers a different plausible fold. This is why candidate count and
candidate diversity must be controlled when source contribution is measured.

\section{Template-based modelling}

TBM is a sequence of algorithms rather than a single copying step. Retrieval finds
known RNA chains that may be related to the query. Alignment maps query residues to
template residues. Ranking estimates which mappings are most useful. Coordinate
transfer copies resolved \cprime{} positions. Gap handling builds positions for query
residues without a template counterpart. Candidate selection finally allocates the
limited prediction slots.

John's public TBM performs an exhaustive scan after a length filter. It computes global
alignment, local alignment, similarity between hand-crafted sequence features, and the
Jaccard overlap of 3-mers, with weights 0.4, 0.3, 0.2, and 0.1. A $k$-mer is every
contiguous substring of length $k$. For sequence `AUGCA', its 3-mers are `AUG', `UGC',
and `GCA'. Jaccard similarity divides the number of distinct shared 3-mers by the size
of their union. Exhaustive search means that the score is computed for every template
remaining after the filter rather than only for a shortlist returned by a search
engine.

\section{Pretrained prediction and candidate diversity}

DRfold2 is a pretrained RNA structure predictor that combines learned sequence
representations and learned geometric information \cite{drfold,drfold2}. In this
thesis, $T$ denotes a raw template candidate and $D$ denotes a direct DRfold2 candidate.
Bank $5T$ assigns all five slots to templates. Bank $3T+2D$ replaces two template slots
with DRfold2 while preserving $N=5$.

Candidate diversity is measured without native labels using pairwise self-TM, the
TM-score between candidates within the same bank. A high self-TM indicates similar
hypotheses. A lower self-TM indicates wider fold coverage, although it does not prove
that any candidate is correct. Near-duplicate pairs are defined using a self-TM
threshold of 0.95. These measures explain a possible mechanism but cannot replace the
native best-of-five score.

\section{Geometric refinement and local accuracy}

A refiner does not generate a fold from nothing. It receives raw coordinates and moves
each \cprime{} point to reduce an objective. The Simple baseline anchors the output to
the source through a Huber loss and smooths adjacent backbone distances. Geometry
Refinement adds collision avoidance, empirical pseudo-angle and pseudo-torsion priors,
and a guard against newly introduced sharp kinks. A source anchor is essential because
the candidate-generation stage may already contain a useful global fold.

SW-RMSD9 evaluates overlapping windows of nine residues. Each predicted window is
superposed on the corresponding native window before RMSD is computed. It therefore
asks whether a short segment has the correct local shape. C1$^{\prime}$-lDDT compares
internal distances under thresholds of 0.5, 1, 2, and 4~\angstrom{} within a local
radius \cite{lddt}. SW-RMSD15, global \cprime{} RMSD, and \cprime{}-lDDT are supporting
metrics.

Angle negative log-likelihood, torsion negative log-likelihood, clash proxies, kink
counts, pair-like fractions, and radius-of-gyration deviations are useful diagnostics.
However, Geometry directly optimizes several of them. A lower optimization diagnostic
only proves that the optimizer reduced its own objective. Independent native-based
metrics are required to support an accuracy claim.

\chapter{Data, Provenance, and Leakage Control}

\section{Kaggle train V2 and data roles}

The Kaggle `train\_sequences.v2.csv' file contains exactly 5,135 RNA records. The V4
master ledger has one row per record and stores the temporal cutoff, sequence length,
normalized sequence hash, native technical validity, prior-training use, development
exposure, exact-duplicate status, regenerated MMseqs sequence-similarity cluster,
model-overlap audit, external-exposure audit, and final inclusion state.

The data flow assigns different roles before performance is inspected. The 3,397 RNAs
with temporal cutoffs before 27 May 2022 reproduce `P0-production', the deployed
distance, size, angle, and torsion priors. These RNAs are prior-training data rather
than final-test data. Phase 1 rebuilt both production JSON artifacts from the exact
3,397-target manifest. Every numeric value matched the deployed artifact, with maximum
absolute error zero at tolerance $10^{-10}$.

The later branch begins with 878 RNAs after the declared structural cutoff. Restricting
length to 30 through 400 leaves 525. Requiring a canonical sequence and a technically
valid native \cprime{} structure leaves 408. Exact-sequence deduplication retains 315
representatives. MMseqs clustering at 80\% identity, 80\% coverage, and coverage mode
zero creates 227 sequence-similarity clusters. Removing 90 clusters with known
development exposure leaves 172 provisional RNAs in 137 clusters.

DRfold2 structural-training overlap screening finds 19 exact overlaps and 75 targets
with an MMseqs homolog at the same 80\% identity and 80\% coverage threshold. Ninety-seven
targets pass this structural-overlap screen. The accessible repository and external
text audit finds no additional exposure evidence for these targets. All 97 targets in
86 clusters are retained without sampling in the final confirmatory manifest.

\section{Development exposure}

The exposure ledger conservatively marks any target whose native performance was
scored, whose per-target result was inspected, or whose result influenced a method,
hyperparameter, source weight, failure analysis, table, or plot. The ledger includes
the 3,397 prior-training RNAs, 107 additional exposed train-V2 targets, and 12 CASP15
targets managed in a separate development ledger. An exposed target and its
sequence-similarity cluster cannot enter the confirmatory set.

The 20-target V4 development cohort comes from a historical calibration split and was
already exposed. It is suitable for component selection but never for confirmatory
claims. The cohort contains 17 regenerated MMseqs clusters. The term
``sequence-similarity cluster'' is used deliberately. MMseqs similarity alone does not
establish a biological family or a shared function.

\section{Temporal filtering and self-exclusion}

A template is allowed only when its release date is strictly earlier than the target
cutoff. Every PDB associated with the target record is excluded from retrieval. The
same filter is applied to John and thesis TBM under the controlled protocol. This
prevents a pipeline from retrieving the answer or a structure that became available
only after the prediction date.

An intentionally unsafe diagnostic is retained only to measure leakage sensitivity.
It allows later templates while keeping the other operations fixed. A higher unsafe
score is evidence that temporal leakage can inflate evaluation. It is not a deployable
method and is never interpreted as an accuracy contribution.

\section{Pretrained-model provenance}

Structural training and sequence-language-model pretraining are treated as separate
provenance streams. The audited DRfold2 source is fixed at commit `3667d6a', and the
cfg97 branch uses 20 checkpoint files recorded by SHA-256. The local structural FASTA
contains 10,432 records and 3,414 unique sequences. The relation between that file and
the public archive is not proven byte-exact.

DRfold2 reports that its RCLM sequence model was trained for 67,000 batches on roughly
30 million sequences from RNAcentral Release 22 \cite{drfold2}. No complete corpus
membership manifest is locally available. The thesis therefore does not describe the
whole pretrained model as time-safe. The final pool passes the structural-training
overlap screen, while unresolved language-model membership remains a limitation.

Boltz cannot be used as primary V4 evidence because the required checkpoint, exact
source state, and training provenance are unavailable. The public John hybrid route
that depends on Boltz is consequently only partially reproducible. This limitation
does not invalidate the controlled TBM comparator or the direct DRfold2 allocation
experiment.

\chapter{Methods}

\section{Public John baseline and controlled variants}

The baseline is named the reproduced publicly released John pipeline. It is not called
the exact winning pipeline because the public notebook, original attached data,
dependency versions, and final competition submission have not been shown to be
identical. Three labels are kept separate:

\begin{itemize}
  \item `J-reported' denotes scores stated on Kaggle or by John and is contextual only.
  \item `J-original-local' denotes the captured public code under the locally
  available artifacts.
  \item `J-controlled' denotes John's algorithm under the same database, temporal,
  exclusion, target, candidate-count, and evaluator rules as the thesis.
\end{itemize}

The public TBM computes the four-part composite score, transfers coordinates through
global alignment, completes gaps, applies a rule-based refiner, and adds jitter. H1
must isolate candidate generation, so its raw boundary is after coordinate transfer
and gap completion but before rule refinement and jitter. `J-controlled-TBM-raw' is
taken at this boundary. A SHA-256-derived seed replaces the public use of Python's
process-randomized hash for deterministic fallback generation. This is documented as
an execution reproducibility change rather than a scientific modification.

The public hybrid notebook routes an entire target to template outputs or to a
Boltz-conditioned DRfold2 branch, then duplicates or falls back when necessary. The
control-flow and fallback behavior are executable locally, but the Boltz-conditioned
structural output is unavailable. J-controlled full hybrid is therefore descriptive
and cannot be used to attribute a component gain.

\section{Common template database}

The controlled template database contains 23,869 valid parsed RNA chains from 8,613
PDB entries. Each chain has a sequence, release date, resolved-residue mask, and
\cprime{} coordinate array. Metadata, coordinates, and per-chain manifests have
separate hashes. John and thesis methods read the exact same snapshot. A target-specific
filter then applies the cutoff date, PDB self-exclusion, and length compatibility.

The length filter uses the absolute length difference divided by the larger length. A
ratio at most 0.6 is allowed when either sequence is shorter than 50 residues, at most
0.2 when either exceeds 1,000, and at most 0.4 otherwise. The relaxed short-RNA rule
avoids discarding partial but useful short templates, whereas the long-RNA rule limits
expensive and implausible comparisons.

\section{Retained thesis TBM}

The final retained TBM is a specific five-stage algorithm selected from the development
ablations.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Eligibility filtering.} Remove templates that fail the length rule,
  have a release date on or after the target cutoff, or belong to a PDB associated
  with the target.
  \item \textbf{Global exhaustive retrieval.} Compute the global alignment component
  for every eligible chain and retain the 50 highest-scoring chains. Ties are broken
  by the stable chain identifier.
  \item \textbf{Alignment and ranking.} Realign each shortlisted chain. Identity is
  the fraction of identical nucleotides in aligned non-gap columns. Query coverage is
  the fraction of target residues that receive a finite template coordinate. Rank by
  identity multiplied by query coverage.
  \item \textbf{Distinct-PDB selection.} Select the highest-ranked chain from each
  unused PDB until five PDBs are represented or the distinct pool is exhausted. Then
  backfill from the remaining ranked chains.
  \item \textbf{Coordinate transfer and gap completion.} Copy finite \cprime{}
  coordinates through the alignment. Fill internal missing spans by linear
  interpolation between the two known endpoints. Extend terminal gaps along the
  nearest backbone direction. Use deterministic de novo coordinates if fewer than
  five candidates remain.
\end{enumerate}

MMseqs, the local-alignment component, hand-crafted features, 3-mer Jaccard, and
template completeness are not part of the retained method. They remain in the RQ1
ablation registry so that their measured effects are reported rather than hidden.
Identity multiplied by coverage is retained as an engineering heuristic because its
development effect is positive but inconclusive. Distinct-PDB selection is retained
as a diversity safeguard, not as an accuracy claim.

\section{Direct DRfold2 candidate generation}

The pretrained branch uses direct cfg97 end-to-end outputs from 20 checkpoint files.
It deliberately stops before the expensive DRfold2 potential-optimization stage, then
converts the two highest mean-pLDDT checkpoint outputs into all-atom structures. The
\cprime{} chain, residue confidence, global mean confidence, and optional learned
distance priors are normalized into a pickle-free candidate contract.

The primary bank comparison uses the same retained thesis template list. $5T$ takes
the first five template candidates. $3T+2D$ takes the first three template candidates
and two direct DRfold2 candidates. If a DRfold2 slot fails, the next frozen template
candidate fills that slot and the target remains in the all-target analysis. The
failure is still recorded as a DRfold2 source failure.

The mechanism banks $2T$, $1T+1D$, and $2D$ help distinguish source strength from
source complementarity. They do not replace the primary fixed-$N$ comparison. Boltz
is excluded because its required artifacts are unavailable.

\section{Simple smoothing baseline}

Simple starts from raw coordinates $X_0$ and optimizes coordinates $X$ for 300 Adam
steps with learning rate 0.04. Its objective contains a source Huber term and an
adjacent-backbone Huber term:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Simple}}=
3.0\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{source}}+
1.0\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{backbone}}.
\end{equation}

The source term is confidence weighted and prevents the optimizer from rewriting the
candidate. The backbone term penalizes standardized deviations from the P0 adjacent
\cprime{} distance. Both use Huber loss so that a small number of severe gaps provide
a repair gradient without dominating every other residue. Simple uses fixed geometry
strength one and disables clash, size, angle, torsion, kink, and pair-context terms.

\section{Selected Geometry Refinement}

Geometry uses the same source and backbone foundation and adds clash, global
pseudo-angle, global pseudo-torsion, and kink-regression terms:

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Geometry}}={}&
3.0\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{source}} + s(c)\big(
1.0\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{backbone}}+
0.3\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clash}}\\
&+0.3\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{angle}}+
0.15\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{torsion}}+
20.0\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kink}}\big),
\end{split}
\end{equation}

where

\begin{equation}
s(c)=0.2+0.8(1-c)
\end{equation}

and $c$ is the candidate's global confidence clipped to $[0,1]$. Low-confidence
candidates receive stronger geometric correction, while high-confidence candidates
remain more strongly anchored to the source. The optimizer runs for 300 Adam steps at
learning rate 0.04 with gradient clipping at 10.

The angle and torsion priors are unconditional global P0 distributions. A
candidate-derived pair-like context was tested but did not beat this simpler control.
The radius-of-gyration term is disabled because it did not show a clear independent
benefit. Fixed geometry strength produced a better SW-RMSD9 point estimate, but its TM
confidence interval relative to production Geometry reached $-0.00526$, just beyond
the preregistered $-0.005$ preservation margin. Adaptive strength was therefore kept.

The kink term does not force every raw sharp angle toward a universal value. It
penalizes a newly introduced or worsened kink relative to the raw source. This limits
local regression without assuming that every sharp native-like motif is invalid.

\section{Failure handling}

Missing output is never silently removed. A missing template slot uses a deterministic
method-specific de novo fallback. A missing DRfold2 slot uses the next frozen template
while preserving the source-failure flag. A numerical failure in Simple or Geometry
returns the corresponding Raw candidate and records the exception. If a complete bank
still fails after every fallback, its primary target TM-score is zero. The primary
population therefore remains the complete frozen manifest.

\chapter{Experimental Design and Statistics}

\section{Primary hypotheses}

H1 compares retained thesis TBM with J-controlled TBM on the same targets, controlled
database, temporal and self-exclusion rules, evaluator, and five raw candidates. The
paired effect is

\begin{equation}
\Delta_{H1}=\mathrm{TM}_{\mathrm{Thesis\ TBM}}-
\mathrm{TM}_{\mathrm{J-controlled\ TBM}}.
\end{equation}

H2 isolates candidate-source allocation. It compares $3T+2D$ with $5T$ using the same
retained thesis TBM, the same targets, five raw candidates, and no refinement:

\begin{equation}
\Delta_{H2}=\mathrm{TM}_{3T+2D}-\mathrm{TM}_{5T}.
\end{equation}

H3 compares selected Geometry with Simple on the same raw $3T+2D$ candidates. For
each target, SW-RMSD9 is first averaged over the five paired candidates. The
superiority effect is written in the positive direction:

\begin{equation}
\Delta_{H3}=\mathrm{SW9}_{\mathrm{Simple}}-
\mathrm{SW9}_{\mathrm{Geometry}}.
\end{equation}

H3 also requires the lower bound of the 95\% cluster-bootstrap interval for
$\mathrm{TM}_{\mathrm{Geometry}}-\mathrm{TM}_{\mathrm{Simple}}$ to be greater than
$-0.005$. If H3 fails either the local superiority gate or the TM preservation gate,
Simple becomes the final refiner.

\section{Candidate-level and bank-level evaluation}

Candidate-level evaluation chooses the native conformation that gives the raw
candidate its highest TM-score. Raw, John, Simple, and Geometry are then compared with
that same locked conformation. A refiner is not allowed to select a different native
reference that favors its own output. Candidate metrics are averaged within target
before targets are aggregated.

Bank-level evaluation follows the competition rule and takes the highest TM-score
over the five candidates and every accepted native conformation. Candidate rows are
not treated as independent samples. The statistical unit is always the RNA target.

\section{Cluster-aware inference}

Each primary hypothesis produces one paired delta per target. The point estimate is
the target-weighted arithmetic mean. A cluster bootstrap samples the $K$ regenerated
MMseqs sequence-similarity clusters with replacement 10,000 times using NumPy PCG64
seed 20260819. Whenever a cluster is sampled, all member targets are carried with it.
The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles form the confidence interval.

The one-sided raw $p$-value uses 100,000 cluster sign flips with the same seed. Every
target in a cluster receives the same random sign. The statistic is the target-weighted
mean delta. H1, H2, and H3 form one Holm step-down family at family-wise alpha 0.05.
The H3 TM preservation interval is a separate noninferiority safeguard outside the
Holm family.

Every result reports the number of targets, number of clusters, mean and median delta,
cluster-bootstrap interval, raw $p$-value, Holm-adjusted $p$-value, and counts of
improved, tied, and regressed targets. An absolute delta below $10^{-6}$ is a tie.
Scores are displayed to three decimal places, while small deltas and intervals retain
four or more decimal places. All decisions use unrounded values.

\section{Confirmatory execution and opening receipt}

The confirmatory manifest contains 97 targets in 86 MMseqs sequence-similarity
clusters, with no sampling. Candidate generation used the frozen commit, database,
priors, DRfold2 checkpoints, fallback rules and per-target seeds. Before native labels
were introduced, the execution produced and hashed 3,011 scientific files totalling
8.940~GB. The completeness gate found 97 TBM banks, 97 raw banks, 97 refinement
factorials, 194 direct DRfold2 candidates and zero generation failures or fallback
targets.

An explicit opening receipt then recorded the output-manifest hash, evaluator code,
US-align binary, local metrics, cluster-aware statistics and the authorization for one
native-label opening. The labels were opened once, all 97 targets were evaluated, and
no method or statistical procedure was changed. The evaluator reported zero scoring
failures. Development and confirmatory results remain separate in every artifact.

\section{Confirmatory RQ1: controlled TBM comparison}

J-controlled $5T$ raw candidates achieve mean best-of-five TM 0.401, whereas retained
thesis $5T$ raw candidates achieve 0.376. The paired effect
$\mathrm{TM}_{\mathrm{Thesis}}-\mathrm{TM}_{\mathrm{J-controlled}}$ is $-0.0255$, a
6.358\% decrease relative to J-controlled. The 95\% cluster-bootstrap interval is
$[-0.0471,-0.0072]$. The one-sided raw $p$-value for the preregistered positive
direction is 0.995940, and Holm does not reject H1. Forty-three targets improve, three
tie and 51 regress.

H1 therefore fails with a negative confirmatory effect. The retained thesis TBM
cannot be presented as more accurate than J-controlled TBM. This conclusion is
stronger than the development result, which had a small positive point estimate but
wide uncertainty. The direction does not generalize.

The preregistered length sensitivity is descriptive. The 30–79 nt stratum is near
neutral at $+0.0007$. The 80–149 nt stratum is $-0.0453$ with an interval below zero.
The 150–249 nt and 250–400 nt strata are also negative but less precise. These
results help locate the failure but do not replace the all-target H1 test.

\chapter{Discussion}

\section{What changed relative to the inherited baseline}

The public John method supplies the baseline ideas of exhaustive template scoring,
coordinate adaptation, rule-based refinement, pretrained candidates, and template
fallback. The thesis contribution is not the use of these ideas in isolation. It is
the controlled replacement and evaluation of specific groups of decisions under a
shared protocol.

The retained thesis TBM is simpler than both the public four-part composite and the
initial thesis pipeline. Development evidence removed MMseqs, full composite scoring,
completeness and curved gaps. Coverage remains a heuristic, while distinct-PDB
selection remains a diversity safeguard. This simplification was a valid development
decision, but the confirmatory result shows that the resulting TBM bank is less
accurate than J-controlled TBM. Simpler does not mean superior, and no thesis TBM
accuracy contribution is claimed.

The candidate bank differs from John's public hybrid routing. John routes a target
mostly to one source, whereas the thesis reserves slots for both sources in every
eligible target. The fixed-$N$ experiment estimates this allocation effect without
rewarding the mixed bank for having more candidates. This is the largest confirmed
TM-score contribution in V4: $3T+2D$ raises mean TM by 0.0342 relative to $5T$.

The refiner is also different from John's three-rule procedure. Geometry uses a
continuous optimization objective with source anchoring and empirical priors. In the
confirmatory same-candidate factorial, Geometry improves SW-RMSD9 beyond Simple and
slightly raises bank TM. The effect is much smaller than the source-allocation gain,
and the unfavorable lDDT result prevents a blanket local-quality claim.

\section{Interpretation of RQ1}

RQ1 produces a negative confirmatory result. Retained thesis TBM is 0.0255 below
J-controlled TBM, corresponding to a 6.358\% decrease. The development cohort did not
predict this direction. The correct interpretation is that development successfully
identified unnecessary internal thesis components but did not produce a TBM bank that
generalizes beyond the controlled baseline.

Coverage and distinct-PDB selection remain correctly labelled. Coverage is an
engineering heuristic selected from development, not a confirmed accuracy term.
Distinct-PDB selection prevents spending several slots on the same PDB evidence, but
its role is robustness and diversity. Neither item rescues the failed H1 group.

The unsafe-date development diagnostic remains important. Future templates raise the
measured score by more than any legitimate development component. Temporal filtering
is therefore part of the scientific method, not merely data cleaning, even though the
time-safe thesis TBM does not beat J-controlled TBM.

\section{Interpretation of RQ2}

The $3T+2D$ effect generalizes. Its 9.098\% relative TM increase is accompanied by a
28.644\% reduction in mean self-TM and a 64.138\% reduction in near-duplicate pairs.
These native-blind diversity changes provide a coherent mechanism: different sources
cover different folds, and best-of-five scoring rewards coverage.

The mechanism is not that DRfold2 is always stronger. The confirmatory $2D$ bank is
below $2T$, while $1T+1D$ is above both. The evidence supports combining independent
sources under a fixed budget. It does not support replacing all templates with
DRfold2, nor does it prove that DRfold2 is uniquely responsible for the effect.

The absence of a reproducible Boltz source limits the mechanism analysis. A matched
Boltz experiment could test whether another independent predictor produces the same
effect. Without that comparison, independent-source complementarity is the strongest
supported interpretation, while the implementation evidence remains specific to
direct DRfold2 candidates.

\section{Interpretation of RQ3}

RQ3 passes on both development and confirmatory data. Its primary evidence is
SW-RMSD9, which uses the native structure but is not optimized directly by Geometry.
Improvements in angle likelihood, torsion likelihood, clashes or kink count cannot
provide the same evidence because these quantities are inside the objective.

The confirmatory SW-RMSD9 reduction is statistically supported but small, 0.473\%.
Bank TM is 0.532\% above Simple and 0.00178 above Raw. Geometry therefore does more
than satisfy a noninferiority boundary on this population, although its TM contribution
is much smaller than the source-allocation contribution. It is a refiner, not a second
fold predictor.

The supporting metrics place an important boundary around this claim. SW-RMSD15 also
improves, while global \cprime{} RMSD and candidate TM are inconclusive. \cprime{}-lDDT
decreases. Geometry therefore improves the preregistered window-based measure and
bank-level TM but does not improve every definition of local structural accuracy.

John fixed further clarifies the trade-off. It gives low windowed error but lowers
bank TM. Local correction alone is insufficient. A competitive refiner must be judged
jointly by an independent local metric and the global-fold objective.

\section{Threats to validity}

The development cohort contains only 20 previously exposed targets. It is appropriate
for component selection but not for confirmatory claims. The separate 97-target set
provides stronger evidence about generalization, and cluster-aware inference accounts
for 86 sequence-similarity clusters. It does not establish performance on every RNA
length, experimental condition or biological class.

The public John reproduction is partial. The original data universe, dependency state,
and exact winning submission are unavailable, and the Boltz-conditioned branch cannot
be regenerated. The J-controlled TBM comparator remains meaningful for RQ1, but
complete-system differences with John are descriptive.

The DRfold2 structural-training screen is stronger than a date-only claim, but RCLM
sequence membership is unresolved. The final set cannot establish complete pretrained
time-safety. Researcher memory also cannot be independently reconstructed; the
external audit can only cover accessible text and artifacts.

The final confirmatory pool uses train-V2 labels that were locally available but kept
unopened for performance until the output freeze and explicit opening receipt.
Procedural separation, hashes and a native-blind runner reduce researcher degrees of
freedom, but they do not create a truly inaccessible external test server. Kaggle
remains the external hidden benchmark for the complete deployed pipeline.

The independent local metrics disagree about Geometry. SW-RMSD9 and SW-RMSD15 improve,
but \cprime{}-lDDT decreases. This disagreement is not treated as a technical failure.
It indicates that a refiner can improve windowed superposition error while altering
pair-distance preservation in an unfavorable way. The thesis therefore avoids a
universal geometry-quality claim.

\chapter{Reproducibility}

The V4 preregistration is frozen at git tag `v4-preregistration-2026-08-20'. The master
ledger contains 5,135 train-V2 rows, while CASP15 targets are managed separately in the
development ledger. Every major artifact records its input path and SHA-256 hash.

The final manifest contains 97 RNAs in 86 clusters. The final freeze records hashes for
the manifest, template metadata, template coordinates, P0 priors, DRfold2 checkpoint
manifest, pretrained provenance audit, selected refiner configuration, statistical
implementation, environment file, TBM pipeline module, and native-blind runner.

The retained TBM implementation is replayed against all 20 frozen development banks.
Coordinates, per-residue confidence, and global confidence match exactly for all 20
targets. The replay reads no native coordinates and performs no structural scoring.

The native-blind execution followed this sequence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item validate every frozen input and code hash;
  \item generate J-controlled and retained thesis template banks;
  \item run direct DRfold2 cfg97 on the frozen final manifest;
  \item assemble $5T$, $3T+2D$, and supporting source banks with the frozen fallback;
  \item run Raw, John, Simple, and selected Geometry cells without labels;
  \item hash the generated outputs and stop;
  \item introduce the native evaluator only after an explicit final-opening receipt.
\end{enumerate}

The execution froze 3,011 scientific files totalling 8.940~GB. It produced all 97
TBM, raw and refined banks, 194 selected DRfold2 candidates and zero generation
failures. Target-level parallel execution was checked against serial execution on
8BU8\_A; all ten refinement arrays were byte-identical. The receipt then authorized
one native-label opening. Evaluation covered all 97 targets with zero scoring failures,
and the result files record the unmodified H1, H2, H3 and supporting analyses.

The result ledger is updated immediately after each experiment. Method descriptions,
results, and interpretations are refreshed from the same frozen artifact rather than
reconstructed from memory. This workflow prevents tables and prose from referring to
different runs.

\chapter{Conclusion}

This thesis uses a competition with a clear best-of-five TM-score objective to study
how retrieval, candidate-source allocation, and refinement affect an RNA 3D prediction
pipeline. The main methodological contribution is controlled decomposition under
shared data and evaluation rules rather than a claim that the complete system was
designed independently of the public winning-team work.

The 97-target confirmatory evaluation does not support H1 and instead gives negative
evidence. Retained thesis TBM is 0.0255 below J-controlled TBM, a 6.358\% decrease.
Internal simplification does not translate into a stronger TBM baseline. Coverage must
remain a heuristic, distinct-PDB selection a diversity safeguard, and the thesis TBM
group an unsuccessful controlled replacement.

H2 passes. Replacing two of five template slots with direct DRfold2 candidates raises
mean TM by 0.0342, or 9.098\%. Lower self-TM and fewer near-duplicate pairs, together
with the matched-$N$ results, support independent-source complementarity. The finding
does not mean that DRfold2-only prediction is superior to template-only prediction.

H3 also passes. Selected Geometry reduces SW-RMSD9 beyond Simple by
0.0205~\angstrom{}, or 0.473\%, and raises bank TM by 0.00218, or 0.532\%. SW-RMSD15
supports the effect, but global \cprime{} RMSD and candidate TM are inconclusive and
\cprime{}-lDDT decreases. Geometry is therefore a small, metric-specific confirmed
refinement contribution rather than a universal improvement in local geometry.

The thesis conclusion is deliberately asymmetric. Candidate-source diversification
is the main confirmed contribution to the competition TM-score objective. Geometry
adds a smaller confirmed effect beyond simple smoothing. The thesis TBM replacement
does not generalize and is not defended as a contribution. Unsupported complexity is
removed, safeguards are labelled accurately, and negative and conflicting results
remain visible. Kaggle is retained as a complete-system external benchmark and is not
used to attribute any component-level effect.
